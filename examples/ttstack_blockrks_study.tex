\section{Introduction}

This report studies adaptive randomized tensor-train (TT) rounding as a function of the
\emph{block-rank parameters} of the sketch, on the two driver problems of Al~Daas et al.\
(arXiv:2511.03598): the parametric ``cookie'' PDE and the Mat\'ern kernel tensor. We reproduce that
paper's experiments but replace its pure \emph{Khatri--Rao product} (KRP) sketch with this package's
\emph{TTStack} recursive sketch.

The thesis is a structural one. The TTStack sketch (block rank $\chi\!=\!N$, with within-block
orthogonalization) is an \emph{oblivious subspace embedding}: its distortion is controlled by the
embedding dimension and the dimension of the subspace being embedded, and is \emph{independent of the
internal TT rank} of the vectors it sketches. The KRP sketch is the degenerate special case
$\texttt{block\_rks}=1$ with raw Gaussian blocks; it carries no oblivious guarantee and, as we
confirm, is \emph{worst for low-rank inputs}. The practical claim we demonstrate: raising the block
rank from $1$ (KRP) to $N$ (TTStack) buys provable, rank-independent distortion control at a
\emph{modest} extra cost, and tames the intermediate-rank blow-up that KRP suffers inside iterative
solvers.

\paragraph{Named methods.} Throughout, a method is fixed by the block ranks of the two sketch
groups (an initial frozen group of rank \texttt{block\_rks} and an extension group of rank
\texttt{block\_rks\_inc}) and by whether the per-block vectors are orthogonalized:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccl@{}}
\toprule
method & \texttt{block\_rks} & \texttt{orthogonal} & description \\
\midrule
strict-KRP  & $1$   & false & raw Khatri--Rao (the paper's sketch) \\
orth-KRP    & $1$   & true  & orthogonalized rank-1 blocks \\
TTStack-$N$ & $N$   & true  & oblivious embedding \\
TTStack-$2N$& $2N$  & true  & wider embedding (cost/accuracy headroom) \\
Adap-R      & $N$   & true  & TTStack-$N$ + a final deterministic rounding pass \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The group-combination weighting is fixed at \texttt{:column} (the empirically chosen default; the
inverse-variance \texttt{:precision} weighting was found biased at small sample counts).

\section{Background: the recursive TTStack sketch}

A recursive sketch contracts each TT core against a chain of small random ``blocks''
$B_k\in\mathbb{R}^{\chi\times n_k\times\chi}$, accumulating a boundary contraction $S\,y$ whose
Frobenius norm is an unbiased estimate of $\|y\|_F$ (Al~Daas et al., Remark~3.1). The block rank
$\chi=\texttt{block\_rks}$ interpolates between a Khatri--Rao product ($\chi=1$) and a full TTStack
embedding ($\chi=N$). The adaptive rounding loop grows each bond's basis until a sketch-based
residual estimate falls below the per-bond budget $\tau = \varepsilon\,\|y\|_F/\sqrt{N-1}$; an
\emph{extension} group of rank \texttt{block\_rks\_inc} supplies additional sketch columns when the
sliding window is exhausted, and the two groups are combined into one unbiased isometry at finalize
time. The single-TT and Hadamard rounding overloads expose both block ranks; the
linear-combination overload used by Sum+Round GMRES exposes \texttt{block\_rks} only.

\section{Provable embedding: distortion vs.\ block rank}

We measure the distortion of the boundary norm estimate $\mathrm{est}=\|S\,y\|_F/\|y\|_F$ (the
quantity that sets $\tau$) over many independent sketches. All methods are unbiased
($\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{est}^2]=1$); the discriminator is the variance/coefficient of variation (CoV)
at a fixed embedding dimension.

\paragraph{E1 --- estimate vs.\ embedding dimension} (mirror of \texttt{NormEstimation.m}). On the
synthetic rank-50 tensor, the Mat\'ern tensor, and a cookie vector, the estimate concentrates on
$1$ as the number of sketch columns grows; TTStack's band is tighter than KRP's at every embedding

\section{Single-TT rounding (synthetic and Mat\'ern)}

Randomized rounding beats deterministic when the \emph{QR/SVD sweep} is the bottleneck: the
deterministic sweep costs $O(d\,r^3)$ per bond \emph{regardless of the target rank}, while the
sketched sweep costs $O(d\,r^2\,\ell)$ with $\ell$ the sketch (target) rank --- a ratio $\ell/r$. (An
earlier implementation of the randomized left-update used a 3-factor \texttt{@tensoropt} that
materialised an $O(d^2 r)$ intermediate, which masked this advantage entirely; rewriting it as two
explicit gemms restored the expected scaling, and the figures below reflect that.)

\paragraph{Synthetic, fixed target rank} (paper \S4.1). Fixed-rank randomized rounding of a
rank-50 + $10^{-5}$ tensor (effective rank $\approx$ TT rank), sweeping the maximum target rank. The
error collapses once the target rank reaches the signal rank; the randomized methods track the
deterministic baseline's accuracy. Even here, where $\ell$ approaches $r$, the randomized rounding is
faster than the deterministic SVD throughout --- $\sim$20$\times$ at the smallest target and decaying
toward $\sim$2.5$\times$ as $\mathrm{rmax}\to r$ (less of the $O(d r^3)$ sweep left to save).
\fig{../out/synthetic_blockrks_sweep/synthetic_blockrks_sweep_signal50.pdf}{synthetic\_blockrks\_sweep.jl}

\paragraph{The rank-gap regime: where the speedup is largest.} The advantage grows with the gap
$r/\ell$. We make it large with a low-effective-rank tensor inside a high TT rank: a rank-4 signal
plus a rank-100, amplitude-$10^{-6}$ perturbation (TT rank $\approx 104$). At the natural target
$\mathrm{rmax}=4$ this is also KRP's worst accuracy case (a low-rank signal):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
method (at rmax $=4$, TT rank $\approx 104$) & speedup vs.\ det & relative error \\
\midrule
deterministic & $1\times$ & $1.0\times10^{-6}$ \\
strict-KRP    & $36\times$ & $3.2\times10^{-5}$ \\
TTStack-$N$   & $29\times$ & $\mathbf{3.3\times10^{-6}}$ \\
TTStack-$2N$  & $17\times$ & $3.1\times10^{-6}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
All variants are now an order of magnitude faster than deterministic; the cheaper KRP sketch is
marginally faster than TTStack, but TTStack is $\sim$10$\times$ \emph{more accurate} at this low-rank
signal --- so the block-rank cost buys accuracy, not speed, exactly where KRP is weakest. As
$\mathrm{rmax}$ grows past the effective rank one over-resolves (the sketch rank approaches the TT
rank), so the speedup fades from $\sim$37$\times$ to $\sim$2.5$\times$ --- the figure shows this crossover.
\fig{../out/synthetic_blockrks_sweep/synthetic_blockrks_sweep_rankgap.pdf}{synthetic\_blockrks\_sweep.jl}
The same $r/\ell$ effect appears at scale on the Mat\'ern tensor (\S4 below) and the cookie solves
(\S5, large deterministic baseline).

\paragraph{Mat\'ern, tolerance sweep} (paper \S4.2 / Fig.~5). Adaptive rounding of the rank-621
Mat\'ern tensor across tolerances. The four-panel figure reports achieved rank, achieved error, the
per-bond rank profile, and wall time; the three-panel figure reports accuracy, speedup over the
deterministic baseline, and \emph{compression} --- the achieved storage ratio $\sum_k r_{k-1} n_k r_k
/ \prod_k n_k$ (TT core storage over the $100^8 = 10^{16}$ entries of the full tensor), which ranges
from $\sim$$4\times10^{-11}$ at $\varepsilon=10^{-1}$ to $\sim$$5\times10^{-10}$ at $\varepsilon=10^{-7}$
(a $\sim$$10^{10}$ reduction). The Adap-R column shows that a final deterministic pass recovers the
minimal rank while preserving accuracy.

\section{Linear systems (cookie, both solvers)}

\paragraph{Block-rank study at fixed $n$} (paper \S4.3 / Figs.~7--8). The cookie linear system
($n{=}8$ parameter samples, 8 TT modes) is solved with both the faithful Sum+Round TT-GMRES (the
paper's method) and the package's sketched GMRES (with window-sketch caching), for each named method,
against a computed deterministic baseline ($\approx$2370\,s). All randomized variants converge to the
same $\sim$$10^{-7}$ true residual in 15--16 (Sum+Round) iterations: Sum+Round runs in 73--91\,s
(\textbf{26--32$\times$} faster than deterministic) and the sketched GMRES in 21--26\,s
(\textbf{92--112$\times$}). The left panel shows the intermediate Arnoldi-basis rank per GMRES
iteration: all sketch variants stay near the deterministic profile (peak $\approx$720--750 vs.\ the
deterministic 720), with strict-KRP the loosest (a symptom of its higher-variance norm estimate). The
right panel compares wall time across the two solvers.
\fig{../out/cookie_blockrks_study/cookie_blockrks_study.pdf}{cookie\_blockrks\_study.jl}

\paragraph{Parameter-sample sweep} (paper Fig.~6/8). Varying the number of parameter samples
$n \in \{8,16,32,64\}$, with all three solvers run at every $n$: deterministic Sum+Round (recomputed
at each $n$, OOM-guarded), randomized Sum+Round (strict-KRP and TTStack-$N$), and sketched GMRES
(TTStack-$N$). The randomized solves are essentially $n$-independent in iteration count and solution
rank (set by the cookie geometry, not $n$); their cost grows only with the parameter-mode core size.
Because the deterministic baseline is computed at \emph{each} $n$, the speedup panel is a genuine
per-$n$ $t_\mathrm{det}(n)/t_\mathrm{method}(n)$ — both the Sum+Round and the sketched advantage hold
(and grow) across the sweep, with sketched GMRES the fastest throughout.
\fig{../out/cookie_nsweep/cookie_nsweep.pdf}{cookie\_nsweep.jl}

\section{Discussion}

Across all three regimes --- norm estimation, single-TT rounding, and linear-system solves --- the
TTStack sketch delivers the same or better accuracy than the Khatri--Rao product at a modest extra
block-rank cost, and crucially with a distortion that is provably controlled and independent of the
input TT rank. The benefit is largest exactly where KRP is weakest: low-rank inputs (where KRP is
both noisy and biased) and the intermediate iterates of a Krylov solver (where KRP's high-variance
rank estimates inflate the basis ranks). KRP remains adequate for high-rank inputs sketched with
ample samples; TTStack is the safe default when robustness, low rank, or a provable guarantee
matters.
